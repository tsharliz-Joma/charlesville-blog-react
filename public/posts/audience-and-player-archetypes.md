      ## Types of Players


      **The Killer**


      A surprisingly unpopular type of player, fewer than 1% (Janaki K. et al., 2026) the killer finds the most enjoyment in dominating the players around them, they enjoy seeing other people lose, the love being the best at the game. This type of player 'likes acting on players' (Extra Credits, 2015), asserting dominance, causing misery to other players are acts that the killer type takes pleasure in. The Guild leader who runs the town,  the player who monopolises on a good so everybody has to go through them are other ways this player type can manifest (Extra Credits, 2015). 


      **The Explorer**


      The friend who has little interest in beating others or the game itself, points and/or prizes hold little value to this type of player but rather the discovery of new things in the game, easter eggs, repeating tasks as long as it means there is a reward that means something new in the game. Roughly 10% of people (Janaki K. et al., 2026) will enjoy running back and forth through screens to defeat the same enemy and gains coins if it means they can buy the key to that secret door that has something mysterious behind, namely the joy of the new.


      **The Achiever**


      The achievement systems you find in nearly every game played, a feature that is nearly unanimously agreed upon or added into games was to begin with the result of targeting these type of players, it was originally designed to find a low cost way to further engage these types of players.(Extra Credits, 2015) The achiever is the one plays to collect accolades, loves high score lists, anything that acknowledges their adeptness of the game.


      **The Socialiser** 


      The most popular, encompassing around 80% of players, are those who enjoy games for the company of others, the connections they make in helping new players, "Socializers are happy to water someone else’s farm in exchange for new crops for their own farm" (Janaki K. et al., 2026). Think of the friends that mostly only play when their friends are online to play or the friends that will chat and meet new people in game lobbies, the socialiser type might plays to socialise more than playing to beat people, receive achievements or discover hidden gems. 


      ## Bartle's Chart


      **Acting "**Acting in this case, simply means to act upon"  **↔ Interacting "**To act with"


      **Players "**The actual people in the world" ↕ **World "**The game itself"


      Bartle created a graph from which his viewed game design, each of the players types belonged in one of the quadrants, by doing this it allowed designers to focuses on the mechanics and features rather than the individual player types, if i were to try give an example it would be similar to skate board tricks the most effect way would be to segment them. At the core there are two types, Ollie based tricks and Nollie based tricks, if your goal was to learn skateboard tricks the most effect way would be to focus on ollie based tricks and a result, consequently all yours ollie based tricks would be targeted and one can see what specific tricks compliment others, EG. learning a kickflip compliments, your Variel kickflip. In the same way with each of the player types falling within a quadrant of the graph one can see the bi-product of the mechanic, system or dungeon they are adding to the game and the type of players it ultimately affects and not build, and/or create to cater each type specially.
